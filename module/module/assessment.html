14  Assessment

By the end of this page you know what to submit, what passes, what fails and when it is due; every number appears here and nowhere else.

The assessment is one piece of coursework, assessed as pass or fail with a mark out of 100 for feedback. It runs from the first lab of week 9 to the end of week [10 | 11] and is expected to take twelve to fifteen hours across weeks 9 and 10, the four labs of those weeks included. The work is the work of Chapter 7 to Chapter 12; nothing is asked for here that a chapter does not produce.

14.1 What you submit

Two things, which are one repository. The portfolio is the README described in Chapter 12 and the files it links to. The repository is submitted at the tag the README names, so that every link resolves against one commit. Nothing is submitted separately: no archive of screenshots, no document, no slides.

Mechanics Value
Where to submit ________ (the submission system named on Chapter 15)
What to enter ________ (the repository address and the tag name, unless the system takes the repository directly)
Format and file limits ________
Due End of week [10 | 11], at ________
Late work and resubmission ________ (institution policy)

14.2 What passes

A submission passes when all of the following hold.

Participants. At least two people took part in the pilots of Chapter 8, and four is the aim. At least one of them used the application in a mode other than mouse with JavaScript: keyboard only, a screen reader, or JavaScript off. Consent is recorded for each, and no participant can be identified from anything in the repository, its history or its screenshots.

Findings. At least three findings, each with a source a reader can open: rows of the metrics file, lines of the pilot notes, or both. A finding with an accessibility cause names its WCAG success criterion (W3C, 2023).

Fixes. One to three changes to the code, each with a diff, each addressing a named finding. One change done well is worth more than three half done.

Verification. At least [10 | 20] of the twenty regression checks in Chapter 10 completed, with no Level A failure remaining. At least one criterion moved from fail to pass. A re-pilot with at least one participant, two being the aim, in the mode the fix is for, with the before and after figures shown side by side. Both paths, enhanced and plain, still work.

Honesty. A change that did not improve its measurement is reported as such, with what you make of it. Limitations are stated by you, not discovered by the reader.

14.3 What fails

Any one of the following fails the submission regardless of anything else in it.

Fabrication: pilots that did not happen, data that contradicts the claims made about it, or a metrics file with no pilot notes behind it. Fabrication receives zero marks and is referred under your institution's academic integrity process.

Fewer than two participants, no metrics file, no change to the code, or fewer than [10 | 20] regression checks completed.

A personal-data breach, meaning a participant's name, email address, student number or face anywhere in the repository, its history or a screenshot, or a session identifier that is a real name, [costs ten marks and is referred to the module team as an ethics breach | fails the submission]. A browser tab or bookmark bar visible in a screenshot is a lesser fault that costs marks and is noted in feedback.

14.4 The mark

The pass mark is 40. The mark is feedback: it says how far the evidence chains are from closing, not which cells of a rubric were hit.

Band Mark What distinguishes it
First 70 to 100 Every finding has a specific source; every fix references its finding and has before and after figures; all twenty regression checks done with no Level A failure; limitations stated and analysed.
Upper second 60 to 69 Chains complete with minor gaps, such as a source given as "pilot notes" rather than a line; most regression checks done; honest reporting.
Lower second 50 to 59 Chains present but some findings lack a source or some fixes lack verification; regression checks incomplete; a fix that broke parity.
Third 40 to 49 The minimums met and little more: sources vague, verification thin, one fix attempted.
Fail 0 to 39 A fail trigger, or the minimums not met.

14.5 What the marker does, in order

First, the marker opens the README at the named tag and checks that the files it links to exist, that the metrics file has at least two participant codes, that there are three findings, a diff and regression data, and looks for the fail triggers: data that is too clean, notes that are missing, claims that contradict the data, personal data in two or three screenshots chosen at random. A failure here ends the marking.

Second, the marker follows the three links of each chain: from each finding to its data, from each fix to its finding, and from each fix to its verification. How many chains close sets the band.

Third, the marker counts the regression checks completed, the Level A failures remaining and the criteria moved from fail to pass, and moves the mark within the band.

Fourth, the marker reads the limitations and the reflection for honest reporting and checks that the plain path still works.

Last, the marker records the mark, the band, the outcome and two or three sentences of feedback naming what was strong and what was missing. This order is why the README in Chapter 12 is written in the order it is.

14.6 Tools and help

Help from an AI tool with the code is allowed and is cited in the README, naming the tool and the file it touched. The analysis, the findings and the reflection are your own; a submission whose findings were produced by a tool is treated as not your work. Your institution's academic integrity policy applies in addition to this page.

Participants may be classmates and friends, with consent. If you cannot find a second participant, say so in the lab in week 9, not after the deadline. The crit in Chapter 11 is the formative check before you submit; nothing from it is marked.
